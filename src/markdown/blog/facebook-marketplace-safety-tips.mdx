You found the exact couch you wanted for a third of the price. Or someone finally messaged about the bike that's been leaning against your wall for months. Now all that's left is meeting a person you've never seen, whose name you only know from a profile picture.

Buying and selling secondhand is a great way to save money and keep good stuff out of landfills. The vast majority of meetups are completely uneventful: you hand over the cash, they hand over the item, everyone goes home happy. But a little planning goes a long way, especially if you live alone or tend to go to these meetups by yourself.

These **Facebook Marketplace safety tips** work just as well for Craigslist, OfferUp, Nextdoor, or any other app where you end up meeting a stranger to make a trade.

---

## Before the Meetup

Most of the safety work happens before you ever leave the house.

### Look at the Profile

Take a minute to look at the person you're talking to. A profile that's years old, has a real-looking history, and has ratings from other buyers or sellers is a good sign. A brand-new profile with no photos isn't automatically a problem, but it's worth being a bit more careful.

### Keep the Conversation in the App

Messaging inside the marketplace keeps a record of who you talked to and what you agreed on. Be cautious if someone pushes hard to move to a different app, asks for a verification code, or wants payment in an unusual way like gift cards.

### Agree on the Details in Writing

Before you go, settle the price, the payment method, and exactly where and when you're meeting. Fewer surprises in person means fewer awkward moments to manage on the spot.

### Trust Your Gut

If the price feels too good, the story keeps changing, or the other person gets pushy, it's fine to walk away. There will be another couch.

## Choosing Where to Meet

Where you meet matters more than almost anything else.

- **Pick a busy public place.** A grocery store entrance, a coffee shop, or a well-lit shopping center parking lot during the day.
- **Look for a designated exchange spot.** Many police stations and some city halls have marked "safe exchange zones" with cameras. A quick search for your town will tell you if there's one nearby.
- **Meet in daylight when you can.** Evening meetups aren't off limits, but daylight makes everything easier.
- **Avoid your home for small items.** For anything you can carry, there's no reason for a stranger to learn where you live.

### When the Meetup Has to Be at a Home

Big items like dressers, appliances, and sofas don't fit in a parking lot. If you're going into someone's home, or they're coming to yours:

1. Bring a friend if you can, or have someone at home with you.
2. Move the item near the front door or into the garage beforehand so the buyer doesn't need to walk through your house.
3. Keep your phone charged and on you, not in another room.
4. Tell someone the address and when you expect to be done.

That last step is the one most people skip, and it's the one that matters most if something goes wrong.

## During the Meetup

- **Keep it short.** Check the item, make the exchange, and head out.
- **Use cash or a payment method you trust.** Count cash on the spot. For larger amounts, meeting at a bank lets you withdraw or deposit right there.
- **Test electronics before you pay.** A busy coffee shop with outlets is great for this.
- **Stay where other people can see you.** If someone asks you to step around the corner or into their car to "grab the rest," you can politely say no.
- **Leave if it feels off.** You don't owe anyone an explanation. "Sorry, this isn't going to work" is a complete sentence.

## The Step Most People Skip: Telling Someone

Here's the thing about most safety advice: it focuses on avoiding problems, but not on what happens if one occurs anyway.

The old advice is "tell a friend where you're going." That's good advice, but it only works if your friend remembers, notices the time, and actually follows up. Most people send a quick "meeting someone for the bike, brb" text and then nobody thinks about it again.

What you really want is something that follows up automatically if you go quiet. That's what a **safety timer** does. ## A Quick Marketplace Safety Checklist

Before you head out:

1. Check the other person's profile and keep messages in the app.
2. Agree on price, payment, place, and time in writing.
3. Choose a busy public spot or a safe exchange zone.
4. Bring a friend for in-home pickups, or move the item near the door.
5. Set a safety timer with a short note about where you're going.
6. Check back in when you're done.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it safe to meet someone from Facebook Marketplace?**
Most meetups go smoothly, especially in a busy public place during the day. Checking the other person's profile, keeping messages in the app, and telling someone where you're going all make it safer.

**Where is the safest place to meet a Marketplace buyer or seller?**
A busy public spot like a coffee shop or store entrance, or a police station "safe exchange zone" if your town has one. Avoid giving out your home address for small items.

**What should I do if a buyer has to come to my home?**
Have a friend with you if possible, move the item near the door or into the garage, keep your phone on you, and set a safety timer so someone is alerted if you don't check back in.
