Real estate is a people business. You meet buyers you've never seen before, walk them through homes you don't live in, and spend whole afternoons at open houses where anyone can wander through the front door. That openness is the job, and most of the time it goes exactly the way you hoped.

But a lot of that work happens alone. A first showing with a new lead. A vacant listing at the end of a quiet road. Locking up after an open house when the last visitor has finally left. In those moments, the only person who knows where you are might be a calendar entry nobody is watching.

A **check-in service for real estate agents** gives you a simple backup: if you don't check back in when you said you would, the people you trust find out automatically.

---

## Why Agents Need a Backup Plan

Most agents already have a few safety habits. You share your schedule with the office, you park so you can leave easily, and you trust your gut when something feels off. Those habits matter. The trouble is that none of them raise an alarm on their own.

- **Showings run on unpredictable timing.** A 30-minute showing turns into 90 minutes when the buyers love the place, so nobody thinks twice when you go quiet.
- **Vacant homes are quiet by design.** There are no neighbors in the next room and no homeowner coming back soon.
- **Open houses are open to everyone.** You're welcoming strangers into a space you don't control, often for hours.
- **Your day is scattered across town.** Even your own family might not know which address you're at right now.

None of this means you should stop doing the work you love. It just means "someone would notice" isn't a plan. A check-in that expects to hear from you, and speaks up when it doesn't, turns that assumption into something real.

A safety timer is built for exactly the kind of one-off moment agents deal with all day. Before you walk into a showing, set a duration and an optional note, like "showing on Maple St, 45 minutes" or "open house, done by 4pm." Then go do your job.

When you're finished, check back in. If the timer runs out and you haven't, there's a five-minute grace period, and then your contacts are notified. The grace period means a showing that runs a few minutes long doesn't set off a false alarm.

You don't even have to open the app. Text CheckIn More's number in plain language, like "45m showing on Maple St," and your timer starts. If the buyers want a second walkthrough, text "need 30 more minutes." When you're back in your car, text "I'm back." ## Tips for Using a Check-In Service as an Agent

- **Start a timer before every solo showing.** Make it part of the routine, the same way you grab the lockbox code.
- **Add a little buffer.** Set the timer for longer than you expect the showing to take. You can always check in early.
- **Use the note field.** A short note like "vacant listing, Oak Ridge Rd" gives your contacts something useful if they ever need it.
- **Pick contacts who can act.** Someone who can call you, then call your office, is more helpful than someone who can't check their phone during the day.
- **Agree on a plan ahead of time.** Tell your contacts what an alert means and what they should do first.
- **Set a timer for open houses, too.** Use it to cover the stretch from the first visitor to locking the door behind the last one.
